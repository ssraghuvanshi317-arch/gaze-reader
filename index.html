Gaze Reader

Keeps your place while you read, using your front camera.

Camera frames are processed on this device and never leave it. "Mouse / finger" mode lets you test the highlighting without the camera: your pointer stands in for your eyes.

Settings

What helps tracking most is long lines with many words, because the jump back at the end of each line is then big and easy to see. On a phone, read in landscape. Mouse-mode noise adds camera-like jitter so you can feel what the tracker deals with.

How it works, and its limits

The camera finds your irises and eyelids (MediaPipe face landmarks, running on your device). A 13-dot calibration learns how those features map to points on this screen.

A phone or laptop camera can't tell exactly which word you're looking at: expect it to be off by a few centimetres. So the app doesn't trust the raw gaze point. It runs a small model of how people read: eyes hop forward a word or two, sometimes back, and at the end of a line make a return sweep, the big jump back to the start of the next line. It weighs the noisy gaze against that model over many frames, so one bad frame can't move the highlight, but a real return sweep does. The height of your gaze counts for little until the app has learned your offset while you read.

  • Tap or click any line to put the highlight there. Keys: ↓/J next line, ↑/K previous, Space pause, F quick fix.
  • If you move your head or the device, tap Fix (look at one dot) or recalibrate.
  • Long lines track best. On a phone, turn it sideways; on a laptop, keep the window wide.
  • Best results: light on your face (not behind you), device steady, camera at eye level.
  • The word underline is approximate: it shows the rough area of the line you're on, not a precise word.
